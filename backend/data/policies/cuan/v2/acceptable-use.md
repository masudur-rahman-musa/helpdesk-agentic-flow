# Acceptable Use Policy

Document id: CU-POL-006
Version: 2.0
Owner: Head of Information Security
Effective date: 2026-02-23
Review cycle: annual
Revision history: 2.0 clarifies the AI tool rule and reworded the monitoring notice; the rules themselves are unchanged from 1.0.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets the rules for using the systems, accounts, and devices of Cuan Financial Services Ltd. It applies to every employee and contractor with a firm account, on any device that connects to firm systems, in the office or remote. Access to systems is governed by the Access Control Policy (CU-POL-001); this policy governs conduct once access is held.

Cuan is a regulated payments and lending firm. Much of what our systems hold is customer money data and customer identity data. The rules below exist to protect customers, the firm, and the individual user, in that order.

## 2. General principles

Firm systems are provided for firm business. Users act under their own named account at all times and are accountable for everything done under it. Users hold only the access their duties need and request changes through the access request assistant or the IT helpdesk portal rather than borrowing access from a colleague. Anything unclear in this policy is resolved by asking the information security office at infosec@cuan.example before acting, not after.

## 3. Accounts and credentials

Each person has one named account in the form firstname.lastname@cuan.example. Sharing credentials is prohibited in both directions: do not give yours to anyone, including IT staff and managers, and do not use anyone else's, even with their consent. The Password and Account Recovery Procedure (CU-POL-007) sets the rules for passwords, multi-factor authentication, and recovery. Leaving a session signed in on an unattended, unlocked device counts as sharing.

## 4. Email and m365

The firm mailbox and m365 workspace are for firm business. Users must not auto-forward firm mail to external addresses, use personal email accounts for firm business, or move firm documents to personal cloud storage. Sending customer data by email follows the handling rules of the Data Classification Standard (CU-POL-010); confidential and pii class data leaves the firm only through approved channels, encrypted, to a verified recipient.

Phishing and other suspicious mail is reported through the report button in the mail client or to infosec@cuan.example. Do not reply, click, or forward it to colleagues to ask if it is genuine.

## 5. Wiki

The wiki is the firm's internal knowledge base. Staff with wiki.editor write and maintain pages; all staff can read. Customer identity data, account numbers, credentials, and anything classed confidential or pii under the Data Classification Standard do not belong on the wiki. Procedures, runbooks, team documentation, and internal announcements do. Authors are responsible for what they publish and remove pages that are out of date rather than leaving them to mislead.

## 6. VPN and remote work

Remote connection to firm systems goes through the firm VPN with the vpn.user role. Users connect only from firm-managed devices. Split tunnelling, personal VPN products layered over the firm VPN, and connecting from shared or public computers are prohibited. When working in public places, users position screens away from bystanders and never leave a device unattended while signed in. Lost or stolen devices are reported to the IT helpdesk within one hour of discovery.

## 7. Personal use

Limited personal use of email, messaging, and web browsing is permitted where it is lawful, occasional, and does not interfere with work or consume material resources. Personal use never extends to: storing personal files in firm systems at scale, running a private business, personal cryptocurrency activity on firm devices, or any use that could associate the firm with content a reasonable customer would find offensive. The firm may withdraw the personal use allowance for an individual or generally at any time.

## 8. Monitoring notice

The firm logs and monitors the use of its systems. This includes sign-ins, email metadata, web traffic over firm networks and devices, file access on firm systems, and administrative session recordings under the Privileged Access Standard (CU-POL-004). Monitoring is proportionate, serves security and regulatory compliance rather than surveillance of individuals, and only authorised staff read it, for defined purposes. Users should have no expectation of privacy in firm systems beyond what the law grants. Where monitoring data identifies a person, it is handled as pii under the Data Classification Standard.

## 9. Prohibited actions

The following are prohibited on firm systems and devices, without an approved written exception:

Attempting to access systems, data, or roles beyond what has been granted, including testing whether a control works. Suspected control weaknesses are reported to infosec@cuan.example, not probed.

Disabling, bypassing, or tampering with security controls: endpoint protection, disk encryption, logging, mail filtering, or the VPN.

Installing unapproved software on firm devices, or connecting unapproved hardware such as personal USB storage.

Copying customer data to any personal device, personal account, or unapproved service, whatever the amount and whatever the purpose.

Using firm systems for harassment, discrimination, or the creation or distribution of unlawful material.

Uploading firm code, credentials, customer data, or internal documents to public services, including public code repositories and consumer AI tools. Before any AI tool touches firm data, the information security office must have approved that tool for that data in writing. Approval for one tool or one data class does not carry over to another.

Misrepresenting identity: sending mail that appears to come from another person, or altering records to disguise the acting account.

## 10. Customer data handling basics

Every user who touches customer data follows three basics, with the full rules in the Data Classification Standard. Look up only the customers your current task requires; curiosity lookups of any account, including your own family's, are prohibited and are visible in access logs. Share customer data only with colleagues whose duties need it. Delete working extracts when the task ends.

## 11. Reporting and consequences

Users report suspected policy breaches, security incidents, and their own mistakes to the information security office. Self-reported honest mistakes are treated as learning events wherever possible. Deliberate breaches, and any breach involving customer data or the prohibited actions in section 9, are handled under the firm's disciplinary process and may be reported to our regulator or to law enforcement where the law requires. Access may be suspended during an investigation.

## 12. Acknowledgement

Every account holder acknowledges this policy at joining and after each material revision. Holding a firm account constitutes acceptance of the monitoring described in section 8.
